Pick your scope

Refresh, replace or reconfigure

Most kitchens fall into one of three tiers. The jump in price between them is driven almost entirely by whether cabinetry is replaced and whether anything moves.

Refresh

Same footprint, same boxes. New countertops, backsplash, sink, faucet, hardware, and either paint or door refacing. The cheapest visible change per dollar spent.

  • Countertop and backsplash replacement
  • Cabinet painting or door refacing
  • Sink, faucet and hardware swap

1–2 weeks · $12,000–$25,000

Replace

New cabinetry in the existing layout, plus counters, flooring, lighting and appliances. Plumbing and gas stay roughly where they are, which keeps permits simple.

  • Full cabinetry replacement
  • Flooring and lighting package
  • Appliance install and hookup

3–5 weeks · $35,000–$75,000

Reconfigure

Walls come out, the island moves, plumbing and electrical get rerouted. Permits and inspections apply. The biggest change to how the room actually works.

  • Wall removal and structural headers
  • Plumbing and electrical reroutes
  • Permits, inspections and engineering

6–10 weeks · $75,000–$160,000

Single-scope work

Countertops and cabinetry on their own

Not every kitchen needs a full remodel. If the layout works and the boxes are solid, replacing the two surfaces you actually touch every day gets you most of the way there.

  • Laser templating and seam planning before fabrication
  • Quartz, granite, quartzite and solid surface
  • Undermount sink cutouts and edge profile selection
  • Refacing with new doors, drawer fronts and matching veneer
  • Soft-close hardware and drawer box upgrades
  • Backsplash tile installed to the underside of the uppers

Finish Selection Package

Choose your finishes before demo day

Every client works through the same curated book. One option per category, locked in writing, so nothing is being decided while a crew stands in your kitchen.

Cabinetry — Door Styles & Finishes

Framed construction, classic box. Shaker is a flat recessed panel that works in traditional and modern kitchens alike. Antique White is a raised layered panel; Maple Walnut is a raised panel in a deep stain.

Also available Maple Walnut raised panel, plus Natural Box framed (Ocean Blue, Urban Oak, Cypress Green) and European frameless slab fronts (Cosmo Sand, Roma Clay, Piano Grey, Oxford White). Frameless drops the face frame for a wider opening and full access to the box.

Quartz Countertops

Engineered quartz for kitchen, island and vanity surfaces. Non-porous and sealed for life, so there is no annual resealing the way there is with natural stone.

Twelve in the book Also Iced White, Marfitaj, Carrara Miksa, Carrara Breve, Calacatta Belaros and Calacatta Rusta. Signature upgrades (Cristallo and Taj Mahal quartz, or a hand-picked slab from the yard) are slab-selected and priced by the square foot after templating.

LVP Flooring

Waterproof rigid core with an attached pad, for living areas, hallways and bedrooms. Self-levelling is quoted against slab condition once the existing floor is out.

Interior Paint

Six house neutrals that work with every tile and cabinet selection in the book. Ceilings are flat white and trim and doors are semi-gloss white unless you tell us otherwise.

Hardware Finish

One finish, carried through the whole house: entry sets, interior levers, cabinet pulls, bath accessories and ceiling fans.

Kitchen questions

What homeowners ask us

Can I stay in the house during a kitchen remodel?
Yes, and almost everyone does. We set up a temporary station on day one, usually the fridge, microwave, coffee maker and a folding table in the dining room or garage, plus a utility sink where the plumbing allows. Expect to eat out more than usual for 2 to 5 weeks depending on scope.
Is refacing worth it, or should I just replace the cabinets?
Refacing makes sense when the boxes are solid, the layout works and you like where everything sits. It runs roughly 40 to 60 percent of replacement cost. It is the wrong call if the boxes are particleboard and sagging, if you want different drawer configurations, or if you are changing the layout anyway. We will tell you which situation you are in at the consultation, including when the honest answer is that refacing is a waste of your money.
How far in advance do I need to pick cabinets?
Cabinet lead time is the single biggest driver of your start date, currently running 4 to 8 weeks depending on the line. We do not schedule demo until your cabinets have a confirmed delivery date, because a kitchen sitting gutted while it waits on a truck is the fastest way to a miserable remodel.
Do you handle permits for kitchen work?
Yes. We pull permits in the City of Houston and in Harris, Fort Bend, Montgomery and Brazoria counties, and we schedule the inspections. Permits are included in your quoted price. A cosmetic refresh that does not move plumbing, gas or electrical usually does not need one. Anything involving wall removal, a relocated sink or a new circuit does.
What do you find most often once demo starts?
In Houston homes built before 1985, the two most common surprises are galvanized supply lines that should be replaced while the wall is open, and undersized electrical service that cannot carry a modern appliance load. Both get photographed, priced in writing and approved by you before any work resumes.
Can I supply my own appliances or fixtures?
Yes. We will install appliances, fixtures and tile you supply, but our 10 year workmanship warranty covers our labor only, not the product. You are responsible for ordering the right quantity and for anything that arrives damaged or short, which becomes a schedule delay we cannot control.
